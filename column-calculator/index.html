How the Column and Pier Estimate Works

A round column uses π × radius² × height. A square column uses width × width × height. The calculator multiplies that volume by the number of identical columns, then applies the allowance you select.

For a 300mm diameter round column 1m high, the geometric volume is π × 0.15² × 1 = approximately 0.0707m³. With 10% selected, the planning quantity becomes approximately 0.0778m³.

Calculate different sizes separately

The count field assumes identical dimensions. If column diameters, widths or heights vary, calculate each size as a separate group and add the cubic-metre results.

Quantity is not structural design

The calculator does not determine column dimensions, reinforcement, concrete strength, footing connections or load capacity. Columns and piers can be structural elements, so use dimensions and specifications from approved plans or an appropriately qualified professional.

Why add wastage?

Wastage accounts for concrete lost during your project:

  • Uneven ground requiring more concrete in low spots
  • Spillage during pouring and spreading
  • Concrete sticking to mixing equipment
  • Minor measurement variations
10% is the calculator's default, not a construction rule.
Choose an allowance that reflects your measurements and excavation.
