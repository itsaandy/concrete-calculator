How the Slab Estimate Works

The calculator multiplies length × width × thickness after converting every input to metres. A 3m × 3m slab entered at 100mm thick has a geometric volume of 3 × 3 × 0.1 = 0.90m³. If you select a 10% allowance, the planning quantity becomes 0.99m³.

The bag count multiplies the quantity including the selected allowance by 108 and rounds up to a whole bag. In the example above, 0.99 × 108 = 106.92, so the result is 107 bags.

Measure the actual pour

Measure the inside faces of the formwork, not a nominal shed, patio or garage size. Take several depth measurements if the base is uneven. For stepped or irregular slabs, divide the shape into rectangles, calculate each section, then add the volumes.

Enter a designed thickness

The calculator intentionally does not recommend thickness, reinforcement or concrete strength. Those choices depend on the purpose, loads, ground conditions and approved design. Use dimensions from the relevant plans, manufacturer or qualified professional.

Check the ordering assumptions

The 108-bag conversion follows Boral's 20kg Concrete Mix guide. Other products can yield a different volume. The cost range uses an indicative Australian retail range reviewed on 24 August 2026; confirm the local shelf price and obtain a ready-mix quote before ordering.

Why add wastage?

Wastage accounts for concrete lost during your project:

  • Uneven ground requiring more concrete in low spots
  • Spillage during pouring and spreading
  • Concrete sticking to mixing equipment
  • Minor measurement variations
10% is the calculator's default, not a construction rule.
